# Evidence-led analyst observations | Synthetic case study

These examples are written as briefing notes, not as claims about real institutions.

## Observation 01: operating efficiency

**Question:** Did expenses increase at a similar rate to revenue?

The Scotiabank-labelled fictional record goes from revenue CAD 9,000M and expenses CAD 5,200M in 2026-Q1 to revenue CAD 9,100M and expenses CAD 5,300M in 2026-Q2. The expense-to-revenue measure changes from 57.78% to 58.24%, an **unfavourable increase of approximately 0.46 percentage points**.

**Interpretation:** Expenses increased approximately 1.92% whereas revenue increased approximately 1.11%. The cost-growth gap is positive. Investigate the categories of expense, seasonality and revenue mix before considering cuts.

**Do not claim:** inefficient leadership, proven cost overrun, or validated industry benchmarks.

## Observation 02: technology recovery

In the RBC-labelled fictional Q2 record, 10,100 kg of devices were recorded as retired and 9,100 kg were labelled recycled. This yields a recycling rate of about 90.1%. That is above the hypothetical 85% target.

**Interpretation:** This sample's recovery tracking looks comparatively strong. Verify the disposition certificates and definition of 'recycled' before treating it as environmental impact.

**Do not claim:** actual sustainability performance or third-party verified recycling outcomes.

## Observation 03: cash reconciliation

Fictional October income is CAD 3,500, withdrawals are CAD 2,626 and opening balance is CAD 3,518. The calculated closing is CAD 4,392, matching the fictional closing statement. The monthly category budget nevertheless shows groceries CAD 25 over and dining CAD 32 over. This demonstrates why **reconciled is not the same as on budget**.

**Interpretation:** Investigate discretionary purchases and whether budget assumptions reflect realistic spending. A clean reconciliation means the cash movement ties out, not that the spending choices were optimal.

The calculations and tests are available for review. It does not use private bank information or demonstrate real-world business impact."

4. Why is reconciliation required before making a budget recommendation?
5. What does a passed unit test not tell you?
6. Which stakeholders would need to approve the KPI definitions?
7. How would you distinguish a functional requirement from a non-functional requirement?

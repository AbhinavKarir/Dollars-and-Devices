# Technology Refresh & Asset Disposition Controls

An operations controller needs to know whether every recorded retired device has collection and destruction evidence, and whether disposal charges meet the sample budget. Missing or contradictory evidence requires investigation before disposition is accepted as complete.

**Decision:** which assets need follow-up, who should investigate, and which costs can be compared reliably? The hypothetical objective is complete evidence for every retired asset by cut-off. It is a control objective, not a delivered outcome.

| Proposed user | Decision | Evidence request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IT asset manager | Locate unconfirmed devices | Asset IDs, retirement records, collection receipts |
| Vendor manager | Resolve duplicate or orphan evidence | Original receipts and asset references |
| Information security | Review destruction confirmation | Certificates for data-bearing devices |
| Finance | Review disposal variance | Unique evidence-complete receipts and budgets |
| Operations sponsor | Prioritize unresolved issues | Exceptions, owners, ages and next actions |

Disposal budgets and charges are in CAD per asset. Only unambiguous, evidence-complete assets enter the comparable cost cohort. The full sample shows CAD 3,520 charges versus CAD 3,300 budget for 110 assets. CAD 220 is an adverse sample variance, not estimated savings or loss. Excluded receipts require investigation before total programme cost can be reported.

Earlier institution-quarter financial calculations remain a separate learning case. They illustrate KPI traceability; they neither fund nor reconcile to this asset sample and establish no relationship between profitability and recycling.

The process, roles, budgets and data are simulation assumptions. There are no actual stakeholder interviews, real certificates, sign-offs or delivered business outcomes. [Control rules and defect catalogue](asset_controls.md) define reproducible acceptance checks.
